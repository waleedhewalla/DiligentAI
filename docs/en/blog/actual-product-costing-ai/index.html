Your Standard Costs Are Wrong: Actual Product Costing for Egyptian Manufacturers

When energy, materials and the pound move every quarter, a standard cost set once a year hides which products and customers actually make money.

By Waleed Hewalla · 26 September 2026 · 5 min read

Share

Most plants we visit set standard costs once a year and reconcile variances at month-end. That worked when input prices were stable. With energy tariffs, imported materials and the exchange rate all moving within a quarter, the standard cost is often out of date before it is used.

Where the margin hides

  • Energy is allocated by floor area or headcount instead of by machine hours per product.
  • Scrap and rework are booked to overhead, so high-defect products look cheaper than they are.
  • Changeovers and small batches are not charged to the customers who order them.

None of this needs new accounting rules. It needs the production data you already have — machine hours, scrap, energy meters, batch sizes — joined to the ERP cost records every week instead of once a year.

A practical first step

  • Pick your top 20 products by revenue.
  • Pull actual machine hours, scrap and energy for the last 3 months.
  • Compare actual cost per unit with the standard, and rank the gaps by margin impact.
Price the product you actually make, not the one you planned to make last January.
